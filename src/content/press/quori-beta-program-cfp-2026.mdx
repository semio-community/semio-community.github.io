Proposals are now open for the **Quori Beta Program** — the first opportunity for research
teams to purchase a [Quori 2.0](/hardware/quori-v2/) robot and help shape the platform before
it goes into production.

Up to ten (10) full Quori robots will be sold to US-based research sites at a price subsidized
by our National Science Foundation awards: **$12,000 for a full body Quori, or $6,000 for an
upper body (tabletop) or lower body (mobile base) configuration**, shipping within the
contiguous United States included. Selected teams receive two robots for that price — the
prototype they assemble themselves, and a production-grade Quori 2.0 of the same configuration
once the design is finalized.

Selected teams assemble their robots together at the **Quori Community Build Workshop, March
1-5, 2027 in Santa Clara, California**, with hands-on guidance from the Quori Team and our
manufacturing partner [OLogic](/organizations/ologic-inc/). The workshop sits immediately
before [HRI 2027](https://humanrobotinteraction.org/2027/), and participants can optionally
showcase their Quori at the [Semio Community](/organizations/semio-community/) exhibition booth
there.

The program is open to collaborative teams led by university faculty at US academic
institutions; collaborations with non-profit and for-profit organizations are strongly
encouraged. Beyond the robot itself, participants join an active community of early adopters:
regular community meetings, shared feedback on the platform, and a direct line into the design
of the production build.

**Proposals are due Friday, December 18th, 2026 at 11:59pm Anywhere on Earth.**

Full details — eligibility, what to include in a proposal, evaluation criteria, key dates, and
terms — are on the [Quori Community Build Workshop event page](/events/quori-community-build-workshop-2027/),
along with the complete Call for Proposals as a PDF. Questions can be sent to
[quori.robot@gmail.com](mailto:quori.robot@gmail.com), and answers will be posted publicly on
that page.

Quori is a collaboration between the [University of Pennsylvania](/organizations/university-of-pennsylvania/)
ModLab, [Oregon State University](/organizations/oregon-state-university/), and
[Semio Community](/organizations/semio-community/), supported by the National Science
Foundation under Collaborative Grant Nos. IIS-2235042 & IIS-2235043.
