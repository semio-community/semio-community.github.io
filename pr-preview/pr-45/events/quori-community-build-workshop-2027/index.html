Quori Beta Program: Call for Proposals

The Quori project is seeking proposals from collaborative teams who wish to purchase a Quori 2.0 prototype robot and join the Quori Community Beta Program. Selected teams will learn about, assemble, and take home a Quori 2.0 modular robot platform — and will be eligible to receive a manufactured, production-grade Quori 2.0 of comparable configuration when it becomes available.

Proposals are due Friday, December 18th, 2026 at 11:59pm Anywhere on Earth.

The Quori Beta Program

Up to ten (10) full Quori robots will be sold to US-based sites at a reduced price subsidized by our National Science Foundation grant awards. The program has three goals:

  1. Provide early access to purchase Quori 2.0 prototypes.
  2. Collect feedback from the HRI community about the Quori 2.0 prototypes, to inform the final production build that is designed for manufacturing.
  3. Build a collaborative community of HRI researchers as early adopters of Quori 2.0 who can illustrate the benefits of the platform.

Quori has been shaped by the HRI community throughout its development, through interviews, surveys, public demonstrations, and gatherings such as the 2025 Quori Community Workshop. The Beta Program is the next step in that conversation.

What You Get, What You Pay

Proposing organizations select one of three Quori 2.0 configurations:

ConfigurationSubsidized price
Full Body Quori$12,000
Upper Body Quori (tabletop)$6,000
Lower Body Quori (mobile base)$6,000

Subsidized prices include shipping and handling to addresses within the contiguous United States.

For that price, selected organizations receive both:

  • One prototype Quori 2.0 of the selected configuration, which the team assembles at the Quori Community Build Workshop, and
  • One final production / manufactured Quori 2.0 of the selected configuration, upon design finalization and pending follow-on funding.

note

The smart speaker module and the arm module are currently in the design phase and are not part of the Quori robot assembled at the Community Build. Smart speaker capabilities are bundled directly with the head module. Arms are not currently available as an option — we encourage proposers interested in designing or developing those or related modules.

The Build Workshop

The Quori Community Build Workshop takes place March 1-5, 2027 in Santa Clara, California. It gathers every accepted organization in one room to assemble their purchased Quori robot, and connects potential collaborators who will all own a Quori and can learn from each other’s use of and experience with the platform.

At the workshop, selected participants receive:

  • Materials for their selected Quori 2.0 robot configuration
  • Assembly instructions and hands-on guidance from our partners at OLogic and members of the Quori Team

important

Teams selected to participate commit to sending at least one (1) and at most three (3) representatives — for example a graduate student, PI, or collaborator — to attend the in-person Quori Community Build Workshop. Travel, lodging, and per diem expenses are self-funded.

Optional: exhibition booth at HRI 2027

The workshop takes place immediately before, and in close proximity to, the 22nd Annual International Conference on Human-Robot Interaction (HRI 2027). Attendees who assemble their Quori at the Build Workshop can optionally showcase it at HRI as part of the Semio Community exhibition booth, on a rotating basis.

Shipping

Following the HRI conference, we begin shipping each selected team’s assembled Quori to their respective locations.

Who Can Apply

To apply, proposing teams must be led by university faculty within academic institutions in the United States. Collaborations with non-profit or for-profit organizations are highly encouraged. The proposing individual or team must be engaged in scientific research that will advance the field of computing, and will involve activities that explore high-impact applications for computing consistent with NSF’s broad goals.

Because this program provides pricing subsidized by the National Science Foundation, the lead organization purchasing the Quori 2.0 robot must be a U.S.-based entity.

What to Include in a Proposal

We invite proposals from organizations that plan to (a) evaluate or improve something that already exists, or (b) make a new or novel contribution. Contributions can relate to:

Platform / Intellectual Merit

  • Design / Aesthetics — contributions relating to Quori’s design.
  • Hardware / Mechanisms — novel modules that connect to the Quori open modular connector, or new hardware capabilities for existing modules.
  • Software / Autonomy — ROS or other software behavior modules that work with Quori, performance analysis of software capabilities, or developing a benchmark.
  • Studies / Applications — publications demonstrating the impact or effect of using Quori in various domains.

Community / Broader Impacts

  • Funding / Grants — incorporating Quori into an existing grant, or writing a new grant that involves Quori. These are not expected to include the Quori team, only the Quori robot.
  • Events / Dissemination — organizing community events, or showcasing Quori at existing events.
  • Community / Outreach — broadening participation in and around the platform.
  • Education / Curricula — integrating Quori into ongoing or new courses or educational materials.

Other / Miscellaneous

We welcome contributions that do not fit neatly into the categories above — datasets and benchmarks, for example.

note

Proposers are not expected to contribute in all, or even most, of the possible ways listed above. The list enumerates contributions the Quori team is already aware of and considers valuable.

How proposals are evaluated

  • Proposed research — the potential for scientific breakthrough of the proposed research and its planned contribution.
  • Team experience — the team’s track record, experience, and biographies. We plan to select a variety of experience levels, including early career researchers, and encourage all to apply.
  • Resources & utilization — the organization’s available funding, facilities, plans to utilize the robot, and use of the robot across research labs or research domains.

Proposals are evaluated by the Quori Team, who may engage non-conflicted senior computing researchers and independent reviewers to aid the review process. Every effort will be made to distribute the platforms to a diverse set of US institutions and researchers. The Quori Team, in its sole discretion, reserves the right to select all, some, or none of the proposals received, and to make awards without providing explanation for those decisions.

Commitments of Beta Program Teams

By participating, selected teams commit to being active members of the Quori Community: attending regular Quori Community meetings, presenting at those meetings where possible, and filling out regular surveys to provide feedback about the platform.

Participants are also required to:

  • Acknowledge the Quori Team when publishing scholarly papers that report on work done with the Quori platform, by noting that the parts of the Quori platform used in the work were received by the Quori team and made possible via Grants No. IIS-2235042 & IIS-2235043.
  • Inform the Quori team and community about publications that involve the Quori platform.
  • Fill out an annual survey, sent by the Quori Team, for three (3) years after the Quori robot platform is deployed, to assess the functionality, robustness, and usefulness of all aspects of the developed hardware and software.

Technical Support

Quori’s modular architecture is intended to let users maintain and upgrade the hardware without custom maintenance or support personnel. Hardware support is provided at the Build Workshop by our partner OLogic; software support is provided at the workshop by Semio Community. The Quori Community Discord and Semio Community Discord servers are a good way to connect with fellow researchers and receive community-based support. Where feasible, Semio Community will provide additional software support.

Terms, Conditions, and Limitations

  • Proposals can request, and receive, at most one full Quori 2.0 robot.
  • A single organization may receive only one full Quori robot platform. We strongly encourage researchers within a single organization to write collaborative proposals that include many researchers at that organization and provide a variety of contributions back to the platform and community. If we receive multiple proposals involving members of the same organization, we may reach out to request they combine efforts.
  • While proposals will not be distributed publicly, they should not be considered private. Proposals should not include information the authors are not authorized to distribute, such as proprietary, confidential, or classified information.
  • This Call for Proposals does not offer compensation to individuals or organizations preparing a proposal, nor funds to any individuals or organizations for participation in a proposal or project.
  • Assembled Quori robots must primarily reside in the US — that is, remain in the US for the majority of each year.
  • Re-sale of Quori 2.0 prototypes or production builds subsidized through the Quori Beta Program is not allowed for the duration of the Beta Program (5 years after receiving the robot).
  • These terms and conditions apply to both the assembled Quori robot from the Build and the awarded production build.
